The problem with lifetime deals

Lifetime doesn't always mean lifetime.

Plenty of one-time-purchase tools quietly require you to log in every 60 or 90 days, or the account gets swept for inactivity. Multiply that across a few dozen AppSumo and StackSocial deals and it's not a matter of if you lose one — it's when.

"I paid once so I wouldn't have to think about it again." That's the whole point of a lifetime deal — and the whole reason it needs something watching it for you. — The idea behind Perpetua
What it actually does

A vault, a scheduler, and an easier way to fill both in.

Every feature below ships in the current build — this isn't a roadmap.

01

License vault

Store the license key, source site, purchase date, and notes for every deal. Free accounts keep up to 3; Pro removes the cap. Everything lives in a SQLite database under your own OS app-data folder — %APPDATA%/perpetua/licenses.db — not on a server.

02

Keep-alive reminders

Set how often a vendor wants you to log back in, and Perpetua's background watcher and tray icon flag it before the window closes — native OS notifications, even if the app isn't open. A bundled vendor-policy dataset can suggest a starting interval for sites like AppSumo.

03

Autofill from a screenshot

Upload a receipt or confirmation email and Perpetua runs OCR on-device to pull the license key, purchase date, and amount. Nothing leaves your computer, and it only ever fills in fields you haven't already typed.

04

Sites & sources

Track the marketplaces your deals came from — AppSumo, StackSocial, Humble Bundle, and Product Hunt ship built in, and you can add your own.

05

Export, backup, and recovery

Export the vault to JSON or CSV any time, and Perpetua keeps rotated local backups automatically (the five most recent). Set a backup email with your own SMTP relay for account recovery — Perpetua has no mail server of its own.

06

Encrypted cloud backup

Pro can back up the vault to your own WebDAV target (Koofr and similar work), encrypted with AES-256-GCM before it leaves your machine. The key lives in your OS keyring with a one-time on-screen reveal plus an emailed safety-net copy, so a fresh install can restore the full vault before you've even logged in.

Getting started

Three steps, then it runs itself.

1

Create a local account

Email and password, no cloud sign-up — this account only exists on the machine it's created on.

2

Add your licenses

Type them in by hand, or upload a screenshot and let on-device OCR find the key, date, and amount.

3

Let the tray icon watch

Perpetua starts at login and notifies you before a keep-alive window lapses — mark it used, and the clock resets.

How it's built

Local-first, and honest about what that means.

Perpetua is a Tauri 2 desktop app — SvelteKit interface, a small Rust/Axum server running on 127.0.0.1, and SQLite for storage. No telemetry, no account server, no dependency on Perpetua's own uptime for your data to be there tomorrow.

  • — Passwords are hashed with bcrypt before they touch disk.
  • — The vault database is not encrypted at rest by default — anyone with access to your machine's file system can read it. Treat device security as vault security.
  • — Pro activation runs through Polar's customer portal, or an offline key via perpetua mint-key. Your organization ID is public, baked in at build time — it isn't a secret.
  • — Pro accounts can share a vault with one other local account on the same machine. That's it — no cross-device sync, no server in between.
